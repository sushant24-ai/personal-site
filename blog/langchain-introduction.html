LangChain #1: An Introduction

December 15, 2023 LangChain LLM AI

This is the first part of a series about LangChain. I'll introduce its core concepts and help you understand how it fits into the AI application landscape.

Before diving into LangChain, let's understand what we're working with — Large Language Models.

🧠 What is an LLM?

LLM stands for "Large Language Model" — a type of artificial intelligence model designed to understand, generate, and interact with human language at a large scale. These models are trained on vast amounts of text data and can perform a wide range of language-related tasks.

The training process typically happens in two phases:

  1. Pre-training: The model learns foundational understanding by identifying relationships between words and concepts from massive text datasets
  2. Fine-tuning: The model is refined with targeted data and specific feedback to improve accuracy for particular use cases

⚡ The Transformer Architecture

Training data is processed through a specialized neural network architecture known as a Transformer. This is the breakthrough that made modern LLMs possible.

At a high level, a Transformer has two main components:

  • Encoder: Processes input data (like a sentence) and compresses it into a context vector
  • Decoder: Takes the context vector and generates output (like a translation or response)

The magic happens through self-attention — a mechanism that allows the model to weigh the importance of different parts of the input differently. For each word, the model assesses how relevant all other words in the sentence are to it.

📊 Types of LLMs

1. Proprietary Models

These are AI models developed and controlled by companies like OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic. They often offer high performance and are backed by substantial resources. However:

  • Can be expensive to use
  • May have restrictive licenses
  • Internal workings are not transparent (closed-source)

Examples: GPT-4, Claude, Gemini

2. Open-Source Models

These models are freely available for anyone to use, modify, and distribute. They promote collaboration and offer greater flexibility:

  • Free to use and modify
  • Community-driven improvements
  • Full transparency into how they work
  • Can be self-hosted for privacy

Examples: LLaMA, Mistral, Falcon

🔗 What is LangChain?

Now that we understand LLMs, let's talk about LangChain.

LangChain is a framework that makes it easier to build applications powered by LLMs. It provides:

  • Unified API: Work with different LLM providers using the same interface
  • Data Integration: Connect your LLM to databases, APIs, and documents
  • Chains: Combine multiple LLM calls and tools into complex workflows
  • Memory: Give your applications the ability to remember context
  • Agents: Build autonomous AI that can use tools and make decisions

🧩 Core Components

  • Models: Wrappers around LLMs (OpenAI, Hugging Face, etc.)
  • Prompts: Templates for constructing inputs to LLMs
  • Chains: Sequences of calls to LLMs or utilities
  • Vector Stores: Storage for embeddings used in semantic search
  • Agents: LLMs that can decide which tools to use
